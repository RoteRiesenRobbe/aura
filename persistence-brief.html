Accounts & persistence: what is decided

The scope is settled ahead of implementation. This is what will be stored, where that state lives in the running code today, and what is still an open call.

Decided scope

Two storage tiers. Account level holds identity — an anonymous-first secret issued on the player's first visit, with optional email/OAuth linking later — plus the account-wide sacrifice unlocks. Character level holds everything else. The split is not optional: the sacrifice loop wipes a character while its unlocks survive.

Only live players persist. Mobs, NPCs and summons always respawn from their authored definitions, so nothing world-side is stored. This is what keeps the schema small.

The character record uses Actor vocabulary, not a dump of the player struct.

Deliberately not persisted

DerivedStats (recomputed from equipped passives on load) · recipe and milestone registries (content, not state) · combination discoveries (they fall out of the spellbook via the recipe cascade) · all combat-transient state — pending and casting cooldowns, status effects, buffs.

Still open

  • Charges. Do spellbook consumable charges survive death? The item most likely to need schema room. Untradeable is the standing recommendation.
  • Current HP and position on logout. Restore, or reset to full at a safe spawn?
  • One character per account, or a succession. The sacrifice loop implies the latter; whether retired characters stay as rows is undecided.
  • Per-character flag store for quest and NPC state. The interaction system now in flight already names flag_set and quest_advance as future grant kinds, so the bag is coming — cheap to reserve now, expensive to retrofit.
  • Campfire respawn anchor. Keyed by client UUID in the connection-state system, i.e. session-shaped. Persist, or re-bind on login?
  • Character age. Stats.BirthTick is a tick counter and meaningless across restarts; it needs a wall-clock timestamp if age is ever surfaced.
  • Database choice. Open.
Persistence is also the point where the deployment stops being a hobby server: firewall, a database bound to localhost, credential handling, and a backup with a restore that has actually been proven. That work belongs to this step, not to a follow-up.
