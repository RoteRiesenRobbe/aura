Aura — Accounts & Persistence

Bugfix: your campfire remembers you

Point-in-time summary · 2 August 2026 · branch accounts-8a · commit 3c9e17ba

This is a human-facing snapshot, not a maintained document. The authoritative source is docs/plan-accounts-frontend.md §10a. Where this document and that one disagree, it wins.

In one sentence

Standing at a campfire long enough makes it your home, and until today that only lasted until you logged out — now it survives, so logging back in puts you where you left off rather than at whichever starting fire the world felt like.

The bug, as reported

"The character does not spawn at the last campfire they bound to when entering the world through login."

Exactly right, and worth saying plainly: this was not something that broke. It is a piece the previous chunk deliberately left out. Chunk 4 taught aura to remember your level, your abilities and your quests; it explicitly did not teach it to remember your campfire, because at the time nothing placed in the world had a name stable enough to write down.

Why it behaved the way it did

Your campfire bind was remembered per connection. That was enough to survive dying — you respawn at your fire, which has always worked — and enough to survive a dropped connection, because a reconnect picks the whole session back up. It was not enough to survive closing the game, because the connection is exactly the thing that ends.

So on every fresh login the game had nothing to consult, and did what it does for a brand-new character: put you at one of the world's starting campfires, chosen at random.

What changed

Campfires have names now

Every campfire in a zone carries an id — spawnpoint-1, spawnpoint-2 and so on. That is the thing your character's bind is written down as. It has to be a name rather than a location, because a location cannot be edited: if a campfire is nudged a couple of metres in the zone editor, everyone bound to it should stay bound to it, and a stored pair of coordinates would quietly point at open ground instead.

The ids are deliberately called spawn points rather than campfires. Campfires are the only thing you can bind to today, but they will not be the last, and a name that already covers the general case is cheaper than renaming everything later.

Placing a campfire in the editor names it automatically

Nothing to type, nothing to remember. The editor assigns the next number and never re-uses one it has handed out, which matters more than it sounds: if a deleted campfire's name were given to a new one, every character bound to the old fire would silently start waking up at the new one.

Deleting a campfire does not strand anyone

If a fire you were bound to is removed from the world, your bind simply stops meaning anything and you spawn at a starting campfire, exactly as you did before any of this existed. The game then forgets the dead bind rather than failing to look it up once per login forever.

This is the case that had to be got right first. The alternative — refusing to let a character into the world because a campfire they once slept at no longer exists — turns a content edit into a locked-out player.

Binding is saved immediately

Not on the next five-minute autosave. Binding to a fire is something you did on purpose and watched confirm on screen, so a crash a minute later must not send you back to a campfire you remember leaving.

A second bug, found while testing this one

Worth reading even though it was never reported, because it made "my last campfire" wrong on its own terms — no logging out required.

Becoming bound to a fire takes a couple of seconds of standing near it. The game counted those seconds, but it only reset the count when you were near no fire at all. Walk directly out of one campfire's circle and into another's — or teleport between them — and it kept counting the first fire. Since the bind happens at the exact moment the count reaches its threshold, and the count had already sailed past it, the bind never happened again. You stayed bound to a campfire you had walked away from.

The automated test caught this immediately and unmistakably: a character that had stood at the eastern fire for twenty seconds saved its home as the western one, because it had bound to the fire it spawned at during the few seconds before it travelled. The counter now remembers which fire it is counting for, and a different fire starts it over.

How it was checked

  • The full Go test suite, including a round trip through a real database with the new column.
  • 92 browser-side unit tests, five of them new and all about the zone editor keeping campfire names intact — the editor rewrites the whole zone file on save, and a field it does not explicitly know about is a field it deletes.
  • A new end-to-end script that plays the actual game: it binds to the eastern campfire on purpose, because binding to a starting campfire would pass whether the fix works or not. It then leaves to character select and comes back, landing 0.6 units from the fire it bound to and 102 units from the one it would have got before.
  • The three existing account and persistence scripts, re-run one at a time on a freshly started server: 15/15, 21/21 and 3/3.

One thing the automated test cannot see

Whether dying after a login sends you back to your bound fire. Not because it doesn't work — the server log shows it working — but because after a respawn the browser test is still reading the position your character had before it died, and no amount of waiting changes that. A check that reports a working game as broken is worse than no check, so that one is covered by a test on the server side instead, and the reason is written into the script for whoever reads it next.

What this still does not do

  • Show you which campfire is yours. There is no marker on the map and no line in the interface; you find out by dying or by logging in.
  • Bind to anything other than a campfire. The naming scheme is ready for it. Nothing else in the world is bindable yet.
  • Carry a bind across zones. One zone is loaded at a time, and a bind made in another one is treated as no bind — which is the right answer, just not an exciting one.
